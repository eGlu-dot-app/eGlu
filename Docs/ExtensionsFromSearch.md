# What eGlu takes from Search's extensions

eGlu's Chrome extension support is written fresh, shaped for spaces from the start (Docs/ExtensionsDesign.md, decided 21 October 2026). Search, the Mac browser eGlu began as a fork of (github.com/driceroland/Search, MIT, on this repository's `upstream` remote), found most of what goes wrong with Chrome extensions on WebKit first. Each of its commits about extensions is read before the part of eGlu it touches is written, and marked here:

- **Taken**: the fix is in eGlu, as it was.
- **Taken, changed**: the fix is in eGlu, changed for spaces or for Chromium's rules; how is said.
- **Not needed**: it doesn't apply, and why (Search's private tabs, its one window, its bench).
- **Not read yet**.

New upstream commits are added as they land: `git log upstream/main -i --grep=extension` and the commits touching Search's extension files.

Area is from the files a commit touched; "Elsewhere" means only Search's other files (its tabs, its browser, its README). Listed oldest first, 156 commits, as of `upstream/main` b81932c (20 October 2026).

## Passkeys

Search carries passkeys out itself on the Mac, with Apple's passkey entitlement for browsers (`com.apple.developer.web-browser.public-key-credential`), which eGlu asked Apple for on 25 October 2026. eGlu takes its relay before Apple has answered, so it is ready for the day the entitlement is granted; until then no build has it, and nothing of this runs but in a test build's rehearsal (below). What is taken:

- `Mac/Sources/Passkeys.swift` is copied from Search's `Passkeys.swift`, with eGlu's names: the ceremony with AuthenticationServices, the request waiting under the field, the channel. Since the engine split it reaches pages only through the engine contracts (`WebEngineCore`: a replying channel in the app's world, scripts in both worlds, the origin and frame the engine records), never WebKit itself.
- Its checks and its answers are in PadCore, where they are tested (`PasskeyRequest.swift`, `PasskeyReply.swift`, `PasskeyTests`): the request read from the page, the verdict (secure page, focus, the frame's site, the relying party, challenge and user), the origins, the credential's bytes, the CBOR of an attestation object, the PRF results sealed for the page.
- `Scripts/passkeys.js` is Search's `PasskeyRelay.page` and `Scripts/passkeys-bridge.js` its `PasskeyRelay.bridge`, as files of their own, with eGlu's event names and HKDF label. The bridge also says where a field marked for passkeys (`autocomplete="… webauthn"`) is while it has the keyboard, since eGlu has no password list of its own for the passkeys to join: they are listed in a popover under the field (`PasskeyList`).
- `Registrable.sameSite` is copied from Search's `Registrable.swift`.

Each copied file says so at its top. Search is MIT licensed, "Copyright (c) 2026 Office Commun", and its notice is this repository's `LICENSE`.

eGlu's own, not Search's: a test build's rehearsal (`-passkeyRehearsal YES`, Debug builds only), where pages get the passkeys without the entitlement and every answer is made up after the same checks, so the page's side and the checks are tried in the real app (`Tests/passkeys.html`, `Tests/passkeys-rehearsal.py`); and `Tests/passkeys.mjs`, the page's scripts against stand-ins in Node. A hands-off host gets no passkey script, as it gets none of eGlu's: Google's sign-in keeps WebKit's own `navigator.credentials`, which Search found refuses sites other than the app's associated domains.
